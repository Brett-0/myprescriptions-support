Privacy Policy

My Prescriptions for iPhone · Last updated August 2026

Everything you put into this app stays on your phone. There is no account, no server and no analytics. I have no way to see your information, and neither does anyone else.

This policy explains what the app stores, where it is kept, and the only circumstances in which your phone contacts anything on the internet. My Prescriptions is made by Brett Allatt, an individual developer in New South Wales, Australia.

What the app stores

Only what you enter or import:

  • The names of the people you are tracking prescriptions for, and optionally a date of birth, a Medicare number and free-text notes
  • Prescription details: medication name, dosage, quantity, prescriber, pharmacy, dates, the number of repeats and how many have been used
  • The prescription link and token from an eScript SMS, when you share one into the app
  • Notes you attach to a prescription, and a history of changes you have made

Where it is stored

All of it is written to the app's private storage area on your device. That area is only accessible to this app and is covered by the encryption iOS applies while your phone is locked. Nothing is copied to a server, a cloud service or a backup controlled by me.

Medicare numbers are treated differently. They are stored in the iOS keychain rather than alongside the rest of the data, which keeps them encrypted by the system independently of the app's own files. They are never written into the app's ordinary storage.

If you back your phone up to iCloud or a computer, the app's data may be included in that backup, under Apple's terms and your own settings rather than mine. A backup you export yourself from within the app is a plain file and does include Medicare numbers, so keep it somewhere you are comfortable with.

What the app does not do

  • No account, sign-in or registration
  • No server — I operate no service that receives your data
  • No analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking of any kind. The app contains no third-party analytics or advertising code
  • Nothing is sold, shared or disclosed to anyone, because nothing is collected

When your phone does reach the internet

The app makes no network requests on its own. There are exactly two occasions when it loads something, and both are started by you:

  • Opening a prescription link. When you share or paste an eScript link, the app opens that page in a web view so it can read the medication and remaining repeats. The page is loaded from the eScript service that issued it, and your connection to that service is governed by their privacy terms, not this policy.
  • Tapping "What is this for?". This opens the relevant Wikipedia article for the medication name. Wikipedia receives that request as it would from any web browser.

In both cases your stored data is not transmitted. The app is reading a page, not sending anything to it.

Reminders

Expiry reminders are scheduled locally by your phone. They are not push notifications, no token is registered, and no server is involved in delivering them.

Sharing prescriptions into the app

The app installs a share extension so eScript links can be sent to it from Messages. It receives only the link you choose to share, and it runs on your device.

Children

The app is intended for adults managing prescriptions for their household, which may include children's medications. It is not directed at children as users and collects nothing from them.

Keeping and deleting your information

Your information stays on your device until you remove it. You can delete an individual prescription or family member at any time, use Delete All Data in Settings to clear everything, or delete the app, which removes its data with it. Because I hold no copy, there is nothing for you to request from me and nothing for me to delete on your behalf.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever changes in a way that affects this policy, the policy will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
